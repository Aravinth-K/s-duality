\section{Validating the boundary limit}
\label{app:rains-limit}
This appendix justifies the boundary limit used in section~\ref{sec:rains}. The
starting point is \eqref{rains:trace} with kernel \eqref{rains:kernel-product}. Integration over the unit torus $|z_i|<1$ requires $|q|<|v_r|<|q|^{-1}$ (see footnote~\ref{rains:contour}). We want to take the limit $\mathbf{b}\rightarrow \mathbf{b}_B$. The central issue we need to take care of
is the development of a singularity in the integrand that collides with the
integration contour. This issue is not present on the $H \mathbf{b}_B$ side, so
we focus on the primary integral here.

For simplicity take $0<Q<t<1$ and $q=\sqrt Q>0$. Deform the boundary
vector~\eqref{rains:additive-BC} by
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{b}^{(\lambda)}
  =\mathbf{b}_B+\frac{\log\lambda}{2\ii}(0,1,0,0),
\end{equation}
with $\lambda\rightarrow1$ from below. This is equivalent to taking $\mathbf{v}^{(\lambda)}=\left(\frac{t}{q},-\frac{\lambda}{q},1,-1\right)$.
The problematic pole's emergence is due to the coefficient $v_2$, which we have
deformed away from. We will show that the integral is still well-behaved as we
take the limit.

Using~\eqref{eq:jacobi-dictionary}, the deformed one-body weight is
$W_{\mathbf{b}^{(\lambda)}}(a)=R(a)F_\lambda(z)$, where
\begin{equation}
  F_\lambda(z)=
  \frac{\Theta_Q(-z)\Theta_Q(-z^{-1})}
  {\Theta_Q(-\lambda z)\Theta_Q(-\lambda/z)},
  \qquad z=e^{2\ii a}.
  \label{rains:boundary-limit}
\end{equation}
A single factor in the denominator looks like
\begin{equation}
  \Theta_Q(-\lambda z) = (1+\lambda z)
  \prod_{m=1}^\infty(1+Q^m\lambda z)(1+Q^{m}/(\lambda z)).
\end{equation}
This has a simple zero at $z=-1$ when $\lambda=1$ from the prefactor, while the
remaining terms in the product remain regular. Factor out this leading term,
\begin{equation}
  F_\lambda(z)=\frac{(1+z)(1+z^{-1})}{(1+\lambda z)(1+\lambda z^{-1})}
  P_\lambda(z),
\end{equation}
where $P_\lambda(z)$ contains the remaining infinite product terms. The pole
occurs for $z=-1$ so let us focus on the contour $|z|=1$. It is then
straightforward to see that the prefactor term obeys the following inequality
\begin{equation}
  0\leqslant \frac{|1+z|^2}{|1+\lambda z|^2}\leqslant \frac{1}{\lambda}.
  \label{rains:boundary-bound}
\end{equation}

For the remaining $P_\lambda (z)$, fix $\lambda_*\in(Q,1)$ sufficiently close to
one and again consider $\lambda \rightarrow 1$. Then one can show
\begin{equation}
  P_\lambda(z)\leqslant \prod_{m=1}^\infty
  \left(\frac{1+Q^m}{1-Q^m/\lambda_*}\right)^4 <\infty.
\end{equation}
Therefore over the whole contour we have $|F_\lambda(z)|\leqslant C/\lambda_*$.

Finally, $F_\lambda(z)\to1$ for every $z\ne-1$. We therefore have that
$F_\lambda(z)$ is bounded uniformly and therefore, by the dominated convergence
theorem, the limit can be taken inside the integral, resulting in the \(B_n\)
Schur-index integrand.
